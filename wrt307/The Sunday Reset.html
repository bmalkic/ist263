The Sunday Reset: Plan Your Whole Week in 30 Minutes

A six-step routine that turns a pile of deadlines, readings, and “I should really…” thoughts into a calm, realistic week.

Project goals

We created this site to teach one practical habit clearly enough that a first-year student could follow it alone. Our goals are to explain each step in plain language, show what it looks like with our own photos, and let you move through the guide in whatever order suits you.

Who this is for

Our target audience is Syracuse University students, especially first- and second-years juggling several syllabi for the first time, though the method works for any student.

What you need: a notebook or notes app, your syllabi, a calendar (paper or digital), and about 30 minutes.

Home › Steps › Step 1

    Step 1: Brain dump everything

    Set a timer for five minutes and write down every task, deadline, errand, and worry in your head. Do not sort or judge yet. Your brain stops juggling once it is on paper.

    Tip: Include small things like “email advisor.” Small tasks cause the most hidden stress.

    Home › Steps › Step 2

      Step 2: Lock in fixed commitments

      Open your calendar and add everything that cannot move: classes, work shifts, club meetings, and appointments. Then add every deadline from your syllabi for the next two weeks.

      Tip: Color-code classes, work, and deadlines so you can read the week at a glance.

      Home › Steps › Step 3

        Step 3: Rank your tasks

        Go back to your brain dump. Mark each item 1 (due this week or high stakes), 2 (important, flexible), or 3 (nice to do). Then write a rough time estimate next to each one, and then add 25% because everything takes longer than you think.

        Home › Steps › Step 4

          Step 4: Time-block your week

          Drag your priority 1 tasks into open calendar slots first, then priority 2. Match hard work to the hours when you have the most energy. Leave at least two empty blocks as “overflow” for things that run long.

          Tip: Name each block with a specific action, such as “Draft intro paragraph,” not just “Study.”

          Home › Steps › Step 5

            Step 5: Plan a “minimum day”

            Some days fall apart. Write down the three tasks that must happen on a bad day, and nothing else. When life gets chaotic, you do your minimum day and still stay on track.

            Home › Steps › Step 6

              Step 6: Do a Friday check-in

              On Friday afternoon, spend ten minutes asking: What got done? What kept slipping? Move unfinished tasks to next week and adjust your time estimates. Over a few weeks your plans become much more accurate.

              Done! Repeat next Sunday. Back to Step 1 · Meet the team
